Practical Use Cases of CASE in PostgreSQL
1. Salary Categorization: Categorizing employees into Low, Medium, and High salary groups based on their salary.

2. Age Categorization: Classifying people as Minor, Adult, or Senior based on their age.

3. Exam Results: Categorizing students as Pass or Fail based on their obtained marks.

4. Product Pricing: Classifying products as Cheap, Moderate, or Expensive based on their prices.

---------------------------------------------
 
select fname,salary, case when salary>90000 then 'High' when salary between 70000 and 90000 then 'med' else 'low' end as sal_category from employees;   
 
 Imp: when using between , Always write the lower value first and the higher value second.

 Task 2:
  select case when salary >90000 then 'High' when salary between 70000 and 90000 then 'Mid' else 'Low'end as salary_category,count(emp_id) from employees group by salary_category;
  
  Execution of Task 2:
  PostgreSQL logically processes your query like this:
1. FROM: Fetches all records from the employees table.
2. GROUP BY: Encounters salary_category and evaluates the CASE expression for each employee to determine their category.
3. Grouping: Puts employees into High, Mid, and Low groups.
4. COUNT(emp_id): Counts employee IDs separately within each group.
5. SELECT: Produces the final output containing the category and its count.
Important: Although CASE is written inside SELECT, PostgreSQL can evaluate that expression for GROUP BY.

Your mental model is now correct:

FROM → CASE evaluation → GROUP BY → COUNT → Final result
